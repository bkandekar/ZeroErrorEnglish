Teaching Philosophy & Credentials

22 Years in the Classroom. 15+ Years in Competitive Coaching.

Why I created the "Zero Errors" method to help Indian aspirants crack competitive English without memorizing dictionary pages or relying on gut feeling.

Over two decades of standing in front of high school and competitive exam classrooms, I observed a tragic recurring pattern: brilliant students with exceptional quantitative and reasoning skills were failing their cutoff scores purely because of English error-spotting.

When asked why they chose an incorrect option, their answer was always the same: "Sir, it just sounded wrong."

Competitive exam setters (for SSC, IBPS, SBI, UPSC, and State PSCs) know this psychological flaw. They deliberately design sentences where the grammatically correct phrasing sounds awkward to an untrained ear, while the grammatically flawed phrasing sounds smooth and natural.

The Genesis of the "Bracket-Elimination Method"

To cure this reliance on guesswork, I developed the Bracket-Elimination Protocol in 2004. By treating sentence analysis like algebraic factoring—putting parentheses around subordinate clauses, prepositional modifiers, and appositives—the core sentence structure (Subject + Verb + Object) stands out in stark relief.

The Core Tenet: Grammar in competitive exams is not literature. It is an exact logical science governed by finite syntactical laws. Master the laws, and zero errors become inevitable.
